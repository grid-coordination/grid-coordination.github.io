# Comments on the Commissioner Workshop on Interoperability

**California Energy Commission, Docket 24-FDAS-02, Load Flexibility Policy & Planning.** Filed November 17, 2026 by Donald C. Jackson, docketed as **TN 273689**. [PDF](/policy/CEC-24-FDAS-02-Comments-of-Donald-C-Jackson.pdf) &middot; [As docketed](https://efiling.energy.ca.gov/GetDocument.aspx?tn=273689) &middot; [Two Signals, Six Tests](/presentations/two-signals-six-tests.html), filed as Attachment A &middot; [Docket](https://efiling.energy.ca.gov/Lists/DocketLog.aspx?docketnumber=24-FDAS-02)

The grid has two problems and they are not the same problem. Balancing supply and demand across the state is an economics problem, answered by a price that changes hourly or faster. Protecting the transformer and the service drop serving one house is a physics problem, answered by a limit on that customer's whole connection, import and export. Both land on the bill. Today the customer receives neither signal directly.

Six days after the workshop, the Commission published draft Express Terms for flexible demand appliance standards for HVAC thermostats in Docket 24-FDAS-03. Measured against the [six tests](/presentations/two-signals-six-tests.html), that draft passes everything about what the appliance does with a signal and fails the tests that decide who the customer deals with.

## What the comments ask the Commission to do

### What a flexible demand appliance standard should require

<ol start="1">
<li>An OpenADR 3 Virtual End Node in the appliance itself, tested with the appliance disconnected from the wider internet.</li>
<li>A server address the customer can configure, with local discovery attempted before any server beyond the premises.</li>
<li>Every power and energy management capability available locally to the owner, over an open standard local protocol, on terms no worse than the manufacturer grants itself or any third party.</li>
<li>An appliance that satisfies the capability on its own, with compliance never resting on an intermediary.</li>
</ol>

### How the rule should be written

<ol start="5">
<li>One common flexible demand appliance standard, stating the capability for every appliance category and the only place it is stated.</li>
<li>That capability stated as the two signals, a price and a limit, without obliging an appliance to implement demand response operating schedules.</li>
<li>The thermostat standard marking its appliance-independent provisions, which the common standard supersedes when adopted.</li>
<li>Protocol criteria in the rule, protocols named on adoption, and an open route by which any party may submit another.</li>
<li>Certification marks resting on a published specification and a published test procedure, with customer choice among the behaviors actually tested.</li>
</ol>

### What the grid side should publish

<ol start="10">
<li>Utilities publishing prices over OpenADR 3, with the MIDAS server obtaining them as a client.</li>
</ol>

### Capacity limits, which answer a different problem

<ol start="11">
<li>What each kind of signal obliges an appliance to do: a limit binds, a price informs, an agent holds only the authority its customer gave it.</li>
<li>One representation of a capacity limit, with no appliance required to hold the whole service connection under it.</li>
<li>Utilities publishing the capacity limit to the customer, authenticated by the existing utility account credential.</li>
<li>An existing utility account credential sufficient to authenticate, with conformance never conditioned on program enrollment or a manufacturer-issued certificate.</li>
</ol>

## Also in the comments

The MIDAS price server is built on the right principles and should acquire its data the same way it serves it. A certification mark is not interoperability: measured against the six tests, the Mercury Consortium's published EVSE specification fails four of six, advertises response-time classes that appear in no published document, and has never released its conformance test procedure. Legacy bridging belongs in products rather than in the mandate.
